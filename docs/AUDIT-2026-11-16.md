# 🔎 El Cacique · full review (Nov 16, 2026)

> Whole project read: 63 updates, ~25,000 lines, 17 apps, 20 tools, 19 plan documents. Compared with how other
> home labs are built in 2026. *Revisión completa del proyecto.*

## Verdict
**The software is in good shape.** Every script passes the automatic code checks (no errors), nothing private is in
GitHub, every app keeps its data on the right drive, nothing starts by itself, and each new piece was tested before
it shipped. For a home lab built in two and a half weeks by one family, it's ahead of most.

**The weak points are not the code. They are the things around it:** one copy of the family's data, power, a server
on Wi-Fi, and nobody being told when something breaks. Those are what take home labs down.

## Fixed in this review
| What | Tool |
|---|---|
| **No backup existed.** The Vault drive was the only copy of photos, documents and Raíces | `tools/backup.sh` (second copy on the Library drive + settings/passwords/keys + CaciquePhotos database; changed/deleted files kept 60 days) |
| No weekly check-up; battery held at ~100% all day; apps' log files could grow without limit; no drive-health warning; Ubuntu security fixes not automatic | `tools/care.sh` (check-up) and `tools/care.sh --fix` |

## Recommendations, in order
### A. This week, on the IdeaPad (free)
1. **Run the first backup** (`backup.sh --check`, then `backup.sh`) and make it a weekly habit.
2. **Run `care.sh --fix`.** The battery reading going 98 → 95% is normal settling, not damage; the real risk is a
   battery kept full for months. Conservation mode holds it near 60%.
3. **A third copy outside the house** (hurricanes, fire, theft): an encrypted USB drive that lives in the safe and
   comes home once a month for `backup.sh`. Without it, the "3-2-1 rule" every guide repeats is only "2-1-0".
4. **Practice a restore once.** Open one photo from `Library/Backups/vault-copy`. A backup nobody has restored is a hope.
5. **Guests:** anyone on the Wi-Fi reaches the front page (confirmed with a visitor). Each app still asks for its
   login, and Raíces asks for the family code; CaciqueGauges and the message of the day are open by design.
   Real separation (guests can see only the Invitado apps) comes with the ER605.

### B. First purchases (before more computers)
6. **A small battery backup (UPS, ~$70–120)** for the modem, the Deeper, the switch and the drives. In Puerto Rico
   this matters more than anywhere: the laptop survives a blink of the power, but the two USB drives don't, and a
   drive that loses power while writing is how photo libraries get damaged.
7. **A cable for the server** (USB-to-Ethernet adapter, ~$15, already in the network plan). The fixed-address
   trouble of Nov 11 was a Wi-Fi problem; servers belong on cable.

### C. With the new hardware: a simpler plan than three servers in a cluster
8. **Start with ONE strong M720q that runs everything, and make the second one its backup.** A 3-computer Proxmox
   cluster shines when apps must jump between machines automatically; that needs shared storage and adds a lot to
   learn and maintain. For a family: node 1 = all the apps (ask for 32 GB of memory instead of 16), node 2 = backups
   (Proxmox Backup Server) and a spare that can take over in an afternoon, node 3 = later, if ever. Same safety,
   half the complexity, and money left for the UPS and the drives.
9. **Put the Vault inside the computer, twice.** USB drives on a dock are the most fragile part of today's setup.
   In the M720q: two drives that mirror each other for the Vault (if one dies, nothing is lost), and today's USB
   drives become the backup drives.
10. **The gaming PC stays its own machine** (Bazzite + Sunshine, streaming to the TVs with Moonlight, on cable).
    It doesn't need to join the server cluster.
11. **ER605:** separate networks for family, guests and smart devices; names instead of numbers
    (`cacique.home`) for every device; the Deeper stays the first wall.

### D. Software next (in this order)
12. **Tell me when something breaks.** CaciqueGauges shows health, but nobody is watching it at 3 AM. Alerts to the
    adults' phones (through the Home Assistant app) for: drive disconnected, disk almost full, an app down,
    overheating, backup overdue. Other home labs use Uptime Kuma for this; ours can do it with what's installed.
13. **Fixed app versions + one update routine.** Most apps use "latest", so a reinstall months from now could bring
    a different version than the one tested. Pin each version; update one app at a time, after a backup.
14. **Account tests** (ElCaciqueAdmin, then Jay), still the open item from Nov 5.
15. **Names and the padlock everywhere** (needs the ER605): this is what lets the Ledger key protect the home apps.
16. The 90-day test on the new hardware, then the installer's public release, then Disconnect Day.

## What other home labs do that we chose differently (and why that's fine)
- **Remote access apps (Tailscale and similar):** skipped on purpose; El Cacique is home-only until decided otherwise.
- **Single sign-on (one login for every app):** worth it for companies; for six adults the account cards are simpler.
- **Kubernetes, Grafana, security scanners:** learning tools for IT careers, not needed for a family server.
